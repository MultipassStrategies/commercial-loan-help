What Is a DSCR Loan and How Do Lenders Calculate It?

Commercial Loan Help Editorial Team February 10, 2026
The Short Version

DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) measures whether a property generates enough income to cover its debt payments. Calculated as NOI divided by annual debt service. Most 2026 lenders require 1.10–1.25 minimum. A DSCR below 1.0 means the property doesn't cover its own mortgage — triggering denial or requiring equity injection. To improve your DSCR, focus on raising income or reducing the debt amount through capital stack engineering.

What Is DSCR? The Definition.

DSCR, or Debt Service Coverage Ratio, is the most important metric in commercial real estate loan underwriting. It is calculated by dividing a property's Net Operating Income (NOI) by its total annual debt service. A DSCR of 1.0 means the property generates exactly enough income to cover debt payments. A DSCR of 1.25 means it generates 25 percent more than required. Most commercial lenders in 2026 require a minimum DSCR of 1.10 to 1.25 before approving a loan.

The concept is straightforward: lenders want to know that the property you're borrowing against produces enough cash flow to pay back the loan — with a margin of safety. The higher the DSCR, the more comfortable the lender and the better terms you will receive. A DSCR of 1.40 or higher puts you in a strong negotiating position for rate and terms. A DSCR below 1.0 means the property doesn't produce enough income to cover its debt, which almost always results in loan denial or requires the borrower to inject additional equity.

DSCR is used across every commercial loan type — conventional bank loans, CMBS, bridge loans, SBA loans, life insurance company loans, and private credit. It is the first number a lender will calculate, and it is the number that most frequently determines how much you can borrow.

How to Calculate DSCR: Step-by-Step

The DSCR formula is: DSCR = Net Operating Income (NOI) ÷ Annual Debt Service. Here is a worked example using a small multifamily property:

DSCR Calculation — Worked Example
Gross Rental Income $250,000/yr
Vacancy & Credit Loss (5%) −$12,500
Effective Gross Income $237,500
Operating Expenses −$57,500
Net Operating Income (NOI) $180,000
NOI $180,000
÷
Annual Debt Service $150,000
=
DSCR 1.20x

In this example, the property generates $180,000 in NOI against $150,000 in annual mortgage payments, producing a DSCR of 1.20x. This means there is a 20% income cushion above the debt obligation — enough for most lenders, but borderline for conservative banks that require 1.25x or higher.

What DSCR Do Lenders Require in 2026?

Lender requirements in 2026 are tighter than they were during the easy-money era of 2019–2022. When interest rates were near historic lows, some lenders accepted DSCRs as low as 1.10x for stabilized properties. Today, most conventional lenders have moved their minimums to 1.20x–1.25x for low-risk assets and 1.30x or higher for sectors under stress, such as office and retail.

The reason is mechanical: when a loan originated at 3.5% refinances at 6.5%, debt service increases by roughly 50–60% on the same balance. A property that had a comfortable 1.50x DSCR at 3.5% may fall to 0.95x–1.10x at current rates — below most lenders' minimums. This dynamic is at the core of the 2026 maturity wall challenge.

For borrowers, this means DSCR is no longer a check-the-box metric. It is the primary constraint on how much a lender will offer and at what rate. Understanding your DSCR before you apply — and taking steps to improve it — is the most impactful thing you can do to strengthen your loan application.

DSCR by Loan Type: What Each Lender Requires

Loan Type Minimum DSCR Notes
Conventional Bank 1.20–1.30 Most conservative; may require 1.30+ for office
CMBS Conduit 1.20–1.25 Standardized underwriting; debt yield also matters
Bridge Loan 1.00–1.10 More flexible; focuses on exit strategy
SBA 7(a) 1.15–1.25 Business cash flow, not just property income
Life Insurance 1.25–1.40 Most conservative; longest terms
Private Credit 0.90–1.10 Most flexible; will lend in negative leverage with equity
Mezzanine N/A (yield-based) Uses debt yield and equity coverage ratios

The ranges above reflect 2026 market conditions. Each lender type uses DSCR differently. CMBS conduit lenders apply a formulaic approach where DSCR directly determines max loan proceeds. Life insurance companies use the most conservative thresholds but offer the lowest rates and longest terms. Bridge lenders and private credit providers are the most flexible — they often underwrite to a stabilized or pro-forma DSCR based on the borrower's business plan rather than in-place cash flow.

What Happens If Your DSCR Is Too Low?

A DSCR that falls below a lender's minimum threshold triggers several possible outcomes — none of them ideal, but all of them manageable with the right strategy:

  • Loan denial. The most common outcome when DSCR is well below 1.0x. The lender concludes the property can't service its debt.
  • Reduced loan proceeds. A lender may approve the loan but at a lower amount to bring the DSCR into compliance. This means you need more equity.
  • Higher interest rates. Some lenders will approve a lower-DSCR loan at a premium rate, pricing in the additional risk.
  • Required equity injection. The lender may require you to pay down the loan balance or bring in additional capital (mezzanine, preferred equity) to reduce the senior debt amount.
  • Refinancing challenges. This is the scenario driving the maturity wall: properties with compressed DSCRs due to the higher rate environment cannot refinance at the same proceeds. Borrowers face a gap between their existing loan balance and what a new lender will offer.

The maturity wall of 2026 — with $875 billion in commercial loans coming due — is fundamentally a DSCR problem. Loans that were underwritten at 3–4% interest rates now need to refinance at 5.5–8%+. The income didn't change, but the debt service cost did, compressing DSCR across the board.

How to Improve Your DSCR Before Applying

If your DSCR is below the threshold, there are concrete steps you can take before approaching a lender:

  • Raise rents to market levels. If your property has below-market rents, increasing them to current market rates directly raises NOI. Even modest rent increases across multiple units can meaningfully improve DSCR. Provide lenders with a rent roll comparison showing your rents versus market comps.
  • Reduce operating expenses. Review your expense categories for inefficiencies: renegotiate insurance, shop property management fees, implement utility cost controls, and audit maintenance contracts. A 10% reduction in operating expenses flows directly to NOI.
  • Increase occupancy. Vacancy directly reduces your effective gross income. If your property is below 90% occupancy, getting to 95%+ is one of the fastest ways to improve DSCR. Invest in tenant retention, reduce turnover costs, and price vacant units competitively.
  • Restructure the loan with longer amortization. A longer amortization period (30 years vs. 25 years) reduces annual debt service payments, which raises DSCR. Ask your lender about interest-only periods or extended amortization as options.
  • Add mezzanine or preferred equity to reduce senior debt. If you can't raise NOI enough, you can reduce the denominator by paying down senior debt. Bringing in mezzanine debt or preferred equity allows you to reduce the senior loan amount to a level where DSCR meets the minimum.
