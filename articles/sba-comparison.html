SBA 7(a) vs SBA 504: Which Loan Is Right for Your Business?

Commercial Loan Help Editorial Team February 5, 2026
The Short Version

The SBA 7(a) loan is the most flexible SBA program — up to $5 million for almost any business purpose. The SBA 504 loan offers the lowest fixed rates for owner-occupied commercial real estate with just 10% down. Choose 7(a) for flexibility or 504 for the lowest long-term real estate cost. Commercial Loan Help matches you to SBA lenders nationwide who specialize in your industry and deal size.

What Is an SBA 7(a) Loan?

The SBA 7(a) loan is the Small Business Administration's flagship financing program, offering up to $5 million for business acquisition, real estate, working capital, equipment, and buyouts. The SBA guarantees up to 85 percent on loans under $150,000 and up to 75 percent on larger loans, reducing lender risk and broadening access for borrowers who might not otherwise qualify for conventional financing.

The 7(a) program is designed for maximum flexibility. Eligible uses include purchasing commercial real estate, acquiring an existing business, buying equipment and inventory, refinancing existing debt, funding leasehold improvements, and covering short-term working capital needs. It is the go-to SBA product when a borrower needs multiple uses of funds within a single loan.

Interest rates on SBA 7(a) loans are typically variable, tied to the Wall Street Journal Prime Rate plus a spread. For loans over $350,000 — the most common for commercial borrowers — the maximum rate is Prime + 3.00%. With the current prime rate at approximately 6.50% (based on the federal funds rate of 3.50%–3.75% as of March 2026), effective SBA 7(a) starting rates are approximately 6.50%–8.75%. Fixed-rate options are available but less common.

Repayment terms depend on the use of funds: up to 10 years for working capital and equipment, and up to 25 years for commercial real estate. The SBA 7(a) program also includes specialized sub-programs like SBA Express (up to $500,000 with faster processing and 50% guarantee) and Export Express for international trade.

Key Requirements

  • Business size: Must meet SBA size standards for your industry
  • For-profit: Must be a for-profit business operating in the United States
  • Owner equity: Borrower must have invested their own time and money into the business
  • Creditworthiness: Credit score of 680+ preferred; strong business financials
  • Collateral: Usually required for loans over $25,000; real estate, equipment, or business assets
  • Personal guarantee: Required for owners with 20%+ ownership

What Is an SBA 504 Loan?

The SBA 504 loan provides long-term fixed-rate financing for owner-occupied commercial real estate and heavy equipment. It uses a tripartite structure: a traditional bank provides 50 percent of the project cost, a Certified Development Company (CDC) provides 40 percent backed by an SBA guarantee, and the borrower contributes 10 percent as a down payment.

The CDC is the distinguishing feature of the 504 program. CDCs are SBA-certified nonprofit organizations that promote economic development within their communities. They originate and service the SBA-guaranteed portion of the loan (the 40% "second mortgage"). The CDC raises capital by selling SBA-backed debentures, which is how the program achieves below-market fixed interest rates. As of March 2026, SBA 504 debenture rates are: 5.61% for 10-year terms, 5.78% for 20-year terms, and 5.72% for 25-year terms — significantly below conventional commercial mortgage rates.

Terms on SBA 504 loans are typically 20 or 25 years for real estate and 10 years for equipment. The long amortization and fixed rate make the 504 program particularly attractive for borrowers seeking predictable, long-term payments on owner-occupied properties.

Critical Requirements

  • Owner occupancy: The borrower must occupy at least 51% of the property (or 60% for new construction)
  • Job creation: The SBA expects one job created or retained per $90,000 of CDC financing (with exceptions for community development goals)
  • Eligible uses: Purchase of land and buildings, new construction, renovation and improvement, purchase of long-term equipment with a useful life of 10+ years
  • Net worth limit: Business tangible net worth under $15 million and average net income under $5 million for the prior two years
  • Collateral: The real estate or equipment being financed serves as collateral
  • Prepayment penalty: A 10-year declining prepayment penalty applies to the CDC portion of the loan

The 2026 SBA Fee Waiver for Manufacturers

In FY2026, the SBA waived all guarantee fees and annual servicing fees on 7(a) and 504 loans under $950,000 for manufacturing businesses. This includes businesses with NAICS codes in sector 31–33. For qualifying 7(a) loans, the upfront guarantee fee drops to 0%. For qualifying 504 loans, both the upfront fee and the annual servicing fee are waived entirely.

The waiver is effective from October 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026 (FY2026 only) and does not automatically extend to FY2027. For a manufacturer seeking a $750,000 SBA 7(a) loan, this can mean savings of $15,000–$25,000 in fees that would otherwise be rolled into the loan balance.

If you operate a manufacturing business and need financing under $950,000, FY2026 is an unusually favorable window to pursue either SBA program.

Which SBA Loan Should You Choose?

The decision between SBA 7(a) and SBA 504 comes down to what you need the funds for and how important a fixed rate is to your long-term plan.

Choose SBA 7(a) If:

  • You need working capital or inventory financing
  • You want to buy an existing business
  • You need equipment, real estate, and operating funds in one loan
  • You want the flexibility to refinance or prepay without a long-term penalty
  • Your deal is under $5 million and has multiple uses of proceeds

Choose SBA 504 If:

  • You are buying owner-occupied commercial real estate
  • You want the lowest possible fixed rate available
  • You can meet the 51% occupancy requirement
  • You want to minimize your down payment (10%)
  • You plan to hold the property long-term (10+ years)

Many borrowers qualify for both programs. In some cases, a borrower will use a 504 loan for the real estate purchase and a smaller 7(a) loan for the working capital component. Commercial Loan Help can help you evaluate the right structure for your specific situation.
