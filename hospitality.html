What Hotel Operators Are Up Against in 2026

The hotel industry in 2026 is bifurcated. RevPAR is hitting record highs in the luxury and upper-upscale segments, but economy and midscale hotels have seen RevPAR decline — in some markets significantly. This bifurcation makes blanket underwriting useless. A lender who doesn't understand your specific segment, flag, and market is the wrong lender.

Making matters worse, hotels that secured 4–4.5% debt in 2021 are now seeing those loans mature. Refinancing at current rates — combined with rising PIP costs, surging insurance premiums, and labor costs that are rising as the workforce is significantly affected by immigration policy changes — is creating a cash flow squeeze that threatens even well-operated properties.

The Right Financing Tools for Hospitality Assets

Bridge Loans for PIP-Encumbered Hotels

Bridge loans provide short-term financing that allows hotel owners to acquire or refinance a property while completing a Property Improvement Plan (PIP). These loans are structured with renovation holdbacks — funds held in escrow and disbursed as PIP milestones are completed — giving borrowers the capital to meet franchisor requirements without waiting for stabilization. Bridge loans for hospitality assets typically carry terms of 2–3 years with extension options, floating rates, and interest-only payments during the renovation period. Once the PIP is complete and the property stabilizes, the borrower transitions to permanent financing at more favorable terms.

Floating-Rate Debt for Transitional Assets

Hotels undergoing repositioning, rebranding, or operational turnaround often need financing that offers prepayment flexibility — the ability to refinance or sell without incurring significant penalties. Floating-rate loans are ideal for these transitional situations because they typically have little or no prepayment penalty, unlike fixed-rate CMBS loans which carry yield maintenance or defeasance requirements. Floating-rate lenders who specialize in hospitality understand that a hotel's value may increase significantly over a 12–24 month period as operations improve, and they structure loans accordingly.

CMBS for Stabilized Hotels

For stabilized hotels with consistent operating history and strong debt service coverage, CMBS (Commercial Mortgage-Backed Securities) loans offer competitive fixed rates, non-recourse structures, and longer terms — typically 5 or 10 years. CMBS loans are securitized and sold to investors, which allows for standardized and often more aggressive underwriting than traditional bank loans. However, timing matters: 39% of all outstanding hospitality CMBS loans are concentrated in Q4 maturities, which means borrowers approaching that window face increased competition for refinancing. Planning ahead — ideally 6–12 months before maturity — is critical for securing the best terms.

Hospitality Financing FAQ

RevPAR stands for Revenue Per Available Room. It is calculated by multiplying a hotel's average daily rate (ADR) by its occupancy rate — or equivalently, dividing total room revenue by total available rooms. RevPAR is the single most important operating metric in hotel finance because it captures both pricing power and demand in a single number.

Commercial lenders use RevPAR to assess a hotel's income-generating ability, benchmark it against comparable properties, and project debt service coverage. A hotel with rising RevPAR signals strong operations and healthy cash flow. A hotel with declining RevPAR may face difficulty covering debt obligations — which is why lenders monitor RevPAR trends closely when underwriting hotel loans and evaluating refinancing risk.
