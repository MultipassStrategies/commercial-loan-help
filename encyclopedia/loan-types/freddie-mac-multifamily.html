Freddie Mac Multifamily Loans: Programs, Rates, and How to Qualify

Commercial Loan Help Editorial Team March 17, 2026
Key Facts
  • Minimum loan: $1M ($750K small balance)
  • Max LTV: 80%
  • Min DSCR: 1.25x
  • Term options: 5 to 20 years
  • Rate type: Fixed or floating
  • Amortization: 30 years

What Is Freddie Mac Multifamily Lending?

Freddie Mac (formally the Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation) is a government-sponsored enterprise (GSE) that plays a central role in financing apartment properties across the United States. While most people associate Freddie Mac with single-family home mortgages, the agency's Multifamily division is one of the largest sources of capital for rental housing in the country, consistently originating between $50 billion and $75 billion in apartment loans each year. Freddie Mac does not lend directly to borrowers. Instead, it purchases and securitizes multifamily loans originated by its network of approved lenders, providing a reliable secondary market that keeps capital flowing to apartment owners and developers.

Freddie Mac's multifamily mission is rooted in its federal charter, which directs the agency to support the stability and affordability of rental housing nationwide. By purchasing loans from its lender network, packaging them into mortgage-backed securities, and selling those securities to investors, Freddie Mac creates a continuous cycle of liquidity that enables lenders to offer lower rates, longer terms, and more favorable structures than would otherwise be available in the private market. This secondary market function is especially important during periods of economic stress, when private capital sources may pull back from multifamily lending. Freddie Mac's consistent presence helps ensure that apartment financing remains available in all market conditions.

The agency's multifamily portfolio spans a wide range of property types and borrower profiles, from large institutional owners refinancing Class A apartment towers to smaller operators acquiring 20-unit workforce housing complexes. Freddie Mac offers distinct programs for conventional market-rate properties, affordable housing, small balance loans, seniors housing, manufactured housing communities, and student housing. This breadth of coverage, combined with competitive pricing and non-recourse loan structures, makes Freddie Mac one of the most important capital sources for any borrower involved in the apartment sector.

The Optigo Lender Network

Freddie Mac multifamily loans are originated exclusively through the Optigo lender network. Optigo is Freddie Mac's brand for its multifamily seller/servicer program, and it replaced the previous "Program Plus" designation in 2019. Optigo lenders are financial institutions that have been approved by Freddie Mac to originate, underwrite, and service multifamily loans on the agency's behalf. These lenders share a portion of the credit risk on the loans they originate, which aligns their incentives with strong underwriting and careful borrower selection.

The Optigo network consists of approximately 25 approved seller/servicers, including large national lenders such as CBRE, Walker & Dunlop, Berkadia, JLL, and Newmark, as well as several regional firms with deep expertise in specific markets. Each Optigo lender has its own production team, underwriting staff, and servicing platform, which means the borrower experience can vary meaningfully from one lender to another in terms of responsiveness, pricing, and deal execution.

For borrowers, the practical implication of this structure is that you do not apply to Freddie Mac directly. You work with an Optigo lender, who handles the entire origination process: quoting terms, collecting documentation, underwriting the deal, and submitting the loan to Freddie Mac for purchase. Because Optigo lenders retain servicing rights and share in the credit risk, they are motivated to close loans efficiently and maintain strong relationships with their borrowers. Choosing the right Optigo lender is a critical step in the process, as lenders vary in their appetite for different property types, geographic markets, and deal sizes. A matching service like Commercial Loan Help can connect you with Optigo lenders who specialize in your specific situation.

Conventional Loan Programs

Freddie Mac's conventional multifamily programs are the workhorse of its lending platform, serving market-rate apartment properties with five or more units. These programs are available in three primary structures: fixed-rate, floating-rate, and lease-up.

Fixed-Rate Loans

Fixed-rate loans are the most popular Freddie Mac product for stabilized apartment properties. These loans offer terms ranging from 5 to 20 years, with 30-year amortization schedules as standard. Maximum leverage is typically 80% LTV, and the minimum debt service coverage ratio is 1.25x. Fixed-rate pricing is based on a spread over the corresponding U.S. Treasury yield (for example, a 10-year fixed-rate loan is priced as a spread over the 10-year Treasury). Interest-only periods are available for qualifying properties, typically ranging from one to five years at the beginning of the loan term. All conventional Freddie Mac loans are non-recourse, meaning the borrower's personal assets are not at risk beyond the "bad boy" carve-out guaranty that covers fraud, misrepresentation, and other specified actions.

Floating-Rate Loans

Floating-rate loans are priced as a spread over the Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) and are popular with borrowers who plan to hold a property for a shorter period, execute a value-add business plan, or who believe rates may decline during the loan term. Freddie Mac floating-rate loans typically offer terms of 5 to 10 years with full or partial interest-only periods. These loans generally include rate caps, which limit the borrower's exposure to rising rates over the loan term. Floating-rate structures offer greater prepayment flexibility compared to fixed-rate loans, often with step-down or yield maintenance provisions rather than the defeasance requirement common on fixed-rate deals.

Lease-Up Loans

Freddie Mac offers a dedicated lease-up product for newly constructed apartment properties that have not yet reached stabilized occupancy. The lease-up loan provides permanent financing during the initial rent-up period, replacing the need for a separate construction-to-permanent bridge loan. These loans are structured with interest-only payments during the lease-up period and convert to fully amortizing payments once the property reaches a specified occupancy threshold. Lease-up loans are available in both fixed and floating-rate structures and are subject to more stringent underwriting requirements, including lower maximum LTV and higher DSCR thresholds compared to stabilized conventional loans.

Small Balance Loan (SBL) Program

Freddie Mac's Small Balance Loan program is one of the agency's most impactful offerings, specifically designed for apartment properties with loan amounts between $750,000 and $7.5 million. Before the SBL program launched in 2014, smaller apartment owners had limited access to agency-quality financing because the fixed costs of underwriting and securitizing a loan made it uneconomical for most Optigo lenders to pursue deals under $5 million. The SBL program solved this problem by creating a streamlined process with reduced documentation, standardized underwriting, and dedicated SBL lenders.

SBL loans are available in fixed-rate, floating-rate, and hybrid structures with terms from 5 to 20 years. Maximum LTV is 80% for most markets, and the minimum DSCR is 1.20x (slightly lower than the 1.25x required for conventional loans). One of the SBL program's most important features is its nationwide coverage. Unlike some conventional agency products that may be limited to primary and secondary markets, the SBL program is available in tertiary markets, rural areas, and smaller metropolitan areas where workforce housing is concentrated. Freddie Mac has approved a specific group of approximately 20 SBL lenders who specialize in originating these smaller transactions and understand the unique operating dynamics of 5-to-100-unit apartment properties.

The streamlined SBL process typically results in faster closings compared to conventional Freddie Mac loans, with many transactions completing in 45 to 60 days from application to funding. Documentation requirements are simplified, with less emphasis on detailed operating histories and more focus on current property performance, market rents, and borrower experience. For small apartment owners who previously relied on local bank financing or private capital, the SBL program provides a significant upgrade in terms of rate, term, and loan structure.

Affordable Housing Programs

Freddie Mac is one of the largest providers of financing for affordable multifamily housing in the United States, and the agency's affordable lending programs support a wide range of properties and subsidy structures. The Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), which regulates Freddie Mac, sets annual affordable housing goals that require the agency to direct a significant portion of its multifamily volume toward properties serving low- and moderate-income renters.

LIHTC Properties

Freddie Mac provides permanent financing for properties financed with Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTC), including both 4% and 9% credit deals. For LIHTC properties, Freddie Mac offers favorable pricing, flexible underwriting that accounts for restricted rents, and the ability to work within the layered capital structures that are common in tax credit transactions. The agency can provide both first mortgage financing and tax-exempt bond credit enhancement for 4% LIHTC deals, which is a particularly valuable combination for developers seeking to maximize leverage.

Section 8 and HAP Contracts

Properties with project-based Section 8 Housing Assistance Payment (HAP) contracts represent a significant portion of Freddie Mac's affordable housing portfolio. The agency has developed specialized underwriting guidelines for HAP properties that recognize the stability of government rental subsidies while accounting for the regulatory requirements associated with maintaining compliance. Freddie Mac can underwrite to the full contract rent on HAP properties, which typically results in higher proceeds compared to underwriting to restricted or market rents alone.

Preservation and Workforce Housing

Freddie Mac actively supports the preservation of naturally occurring affordable housing (NOAH) through targeted programs that provide pricing incentives for borrowers who commit to maintaining affordability for a specified period. The agency's workforce housing initiatives focus on properties serving renters earning between 60% and 80% of area median income (AMI). These programs recognize that even properties without formal income restrictions can serve an affordable function in their communities, and they offer rate reductions and enhanced terms to borrowers who agree to limit rent increases or maintain income-restricted units.

Specialty Programs

Beyond conventional and affordable apartment lending, Freddie Mac offers targeted programs for several niche property types within the multifamily sector.

Seniors Housing

Freddie Mac finances seniors housing properties including independent living, assisted living, and memory care facilities. The seniors housing program includes specific underwriting criteria that account for the unique operating characteristics of these properties, including higher operating expense ratios, specialized staffing requirements, and the role of healthcare regulations. Eligible properties must have a minimum of 20 units and a demonstrated operating history. Freddie Mac's seniors housing loans are available in both fixed and floating-rate structures with terms up to 10 years.

Manufactured Housing Communities

Freddie Mac provides financing for manufactured housing communities (sometimes called mobile home parks) through a dedicated program. Eligible communities must include a minimum number of pad sites and demonstrate stable occupancy and competent management. Freddie Mac has been a significant source of capital for this sector, which serves as one of the most affordable forms of unsubsidized housing in the United States. Loan structures for manufactured housing communities are similar to conventional apartment loans, with terms of 5 to 20 years and up to 80% LTV.

Student Housing

Freddie Mac finances purpose-built student housing properties located near colleges and universities. The student housing program has specific underwriting requirements, including proximity to campus, lease-by-the-bed structures, and the enrollment stability of the affiliated institution. Properties must demonstrate strong historical occupancy and effective marketing to maintain high rent-up each academic year. Student housing loans are available in both fixed and floating-rate structures, though underwriting standards tend to be more conservative than conventional apartment loans due to the unique lease-up risk associated with annual student turnover.

Rate Structure and Pricing

Freddie Mac multifamily rates are among the most competitive in the apartment lending market, which is a direct result of the agency's access to the secondary market and its ability to securitize loans into highly rated mortgage-backed securities. Understanding how Freddie Mac rates are set helps borrowers evaluate quotes and negotiate effectively with Optigo lenders.

Fixed-Rate Pricing

Fixed-rate Freddie Mac loans are priced as a spread over the corresponding U.S. Treasury yield. For example, a 10-year fixed-rate loan is priced as a spread over the 10-year Treasury, while a 7-year loan references the 7-year Treasury. The spread varies based on several factors: loan-to-value ratio, debt service coverage, property quality and location, market strength, borrower experience, and whether the property qualifies for any pricing incentives (such as green or affordable programs). In typical market conditions, Freddie Mac fixed-rate spreads range from approximately 150 to 250 basis points over the relevant Treasury benchmark.

Floating-Rate Pricing

Floating-rate loans are priced as a spread over SOFR (the Secured Overnight Financing Rate, which replaced LIBOR as the standard floating-rate benchmark in 2023). SOFR-based spreads on Freddie Mac floating-rate loans typically range from 150 to 300 basis points, depending on leverage, property profile, and market conditions. Borrowers are generally required to purchase interest rate caps to limit their exposure to rising rates over the loan term.

Qualification Requirements

Freddie Mac multifamily loans have specific qualification standards that apply to both the property and the borrower. Meeting these thresholds is essential for securing agency financing.

Property Requirements

  • Property type: Multifamily residential properties with five or more units. This includes conventional apartments, age-restricted seniors housing, student housing, affordable housing, and manufactured housing communities.
  • Occupancy: Stabilized properties generally must demonstrate occupancy of 90% or higher for at least 90 consecutive days prior to application. Lease-up loans have different occupancy thresholds.
  • Condition: The property must be in good physical condition or the borrower must present a viable plan and budget for required repairs. Freddie Mac may require an escrow for deferred maintenance or capital improvements.
  • Market position: The property must be located in a market with demonstrated rental demand and stable or growing fundamentals. Freddie Mac underwrites to market rents, so the property's in-place rents must be supportable by comparable properties in the area.

Borrower Requirements

  • Net worth: The borrower (or guarantor on the non-recourse carve-out) must have a net worth equal to or greater than the loan amount. For larger deals, the net worth requirement may be set at a percentage of the loan balance.
  • Liquidity: The borrower must demonstrate post-closing liquidity equal to at least 10% of the loan amount. Liquid assets include cash, marketable securities, and unencumbered real estate equity (with appropriate discounting).
  • Experience: Freddie Mac requires that the borrower or a member of the ownership group have demonstrated experience in owning and managing multifamily properties of similar size and complexity. First-time apartment buyers may qualify by partnering with an experienced operator or property management company.
  • Credit history: The borrower must have a clean credit history with no recent bankruptcies, foreclosures, or deeds-in-lieu. Material litigation, unresolved tax liens, and delinquent federal obligations can also disqualify a borrower.

Financial Metrics

  • Maximum LTV: 80% for conventional loans (lower for certain property types and higher-risk profiles).
  • Minimum DSCR: 1.25x for conventional loans, 1.20x for SBL loans. The DSCR is calculated using underwritten net operating income divided by the annual debt service payment (including both principal and interest on an amortizing basis).
  • Amortization: 30 years is standard. Interest-only periods of one to five years are available for qualifying properties and typically require a lower LTV or higher DSCR.

Green Up and Green Up Plus Programs

Freddie Mac has been a leader in green multifamily lending, and its Green Up and Green Up Plus programs incentivize energy and water efficiency improvements by offering interest rate reductions to borrowers who commit to measurable sustainability goals.

Green Up

The Green Up program is available to borrowers who commit to making energy or water efficiency improvements within 12 months of loan closing. To qualify, the planned improvements must be projected to achieve a reduction of at least 15% in energy or water consumption at the property. Common qualifying improvements include LED lighting upgrades, low-flow water fixtures, HVAC system replacements, insulation and weatherization, and ENERGY STAR-rated appliances. Borrowers who participate in Green Up receive a rate reduction of approximately 10 basis points on their loan, which translates into meaningful savings over a 10- or 20-year term. Freddie Mac requires a Green Assessment at origination to establish baseline consumption and verify that the proposed improvements will meet the 15% threshold.

Green Up Plus

Green Up Plus provides a larger rate reduction for borrowers who commit to more significant sustainability achievements. Qualifying criteria include achieving a recognized green building certification (such as ENERGY STAR, LEED, National Green Building Standard, or an equivalent) or demonstrating a projected 30% or greater reduction in energy or water consumption. The rate reduction under Green Up Plus is typically 15 to 25 basis points, depending on the scope of the improvements and the certification level targeted. Properties that have already achieved green certification may qualify for Green Up Plus pricing at origination without additional improvement commitments.

Both Green Up and Green Up Plus are available across all Freddie Mac multifamily loan products, including conventional, SBL, and affordable housing loans. The combined benefit of lower interest rates and reduced operating costs from energy efficiency improvements creates a compelling financial case for participation. Freddie Mac has financed over $100 billion in green multifamily loans since launching these programs, making it one of the largest green bond issuers in the world.

Application Process and Timeline

The Freddie Mac multifamily loan process follows a structured path from initial inquiry through closing. While timelines vary by deal complexity and lender workload, borrowers should expect the following general sequence.

  1. Lender selection and initial screening. The borrower identifies and contacts one or more Optigo lenders (or uses a matching service to connect with appropriate lenders). The lender performs a preliminary review of the property, borrower financials, and deal structure to determine whether the transaction fits within Freddie Mac guidelines.
  2. Term sheet and application. If the initial screening is favorable, the lender issues a term sheet (also called an application or quote letter) that outlines the proposed loan amount, rate, term, amortization, prepayment structure, and key conditions. The borrower reviews and executes the term sheet, which typically requires a good-faith deposit (usually $10,000 to $50,000) to cover third-party report costs.
  3. Due diligence and underwriting. The lender orders third-party reports including an appraisal, property condition assessment, Phase I environmental site assessment, and a seismic report (if applicable). Simultaneously, the lender collects and reviews borrower financial statements, property operating statements, rent rolls, tax returns, and organizational documents. The lender's underwriting team analyzes the deal and prepares a credit package for internal approval.
  4. Lender approval and Freddie Mac submission. Once the lender's credit committee approves the loan, the complete package is submitted to Freddie Mac for review and purchase commitment. Freddie Mac evaluates the underwriting, verifies compliance with its guidelines, and issues a commitment letter.
  5. Closing and funding. After Freddie Mac issues its commitment, the lender's closing team coordinates with legal counsel, title companies, and the borrower to execute loan documents and fund the loan. Freddie Mac purchases the loan from the Optigo lender, and the lender retains the servicing relationship for the life of the loan.

Benefits and Limitations

Benefits

  • Competitive rates. Freddie Mac's access to the capital markets and its GSE status allow it to offer rates that are consistently among the lowest available for multifamily financing. The combination of low spreads and non-recourse structure makes agency lending the benchmark for apartment finance.
  • Non-recourse structure. All standard Freddie Mac multifamily loans are non-recourse, meaning the borrower's personal assets are not at risk beyond the limited "bad boy" carve-out guaranty. This is a significant advantage over bank and credit union financing, which typically requires full personal recourse.
  • Long-term fixed rates. With fixed-rate terms available up to 20 years, Freddie Mac provides the rate certainty that apartment owners need to plan cash flow and investment returns over extended holding periods.
  • Interest-only options. Qualifying borrowers can obtain interest-only periods of one to five years, which improves near-term cash flow and can enhance equity returns during the early years of ownership.
  • Supplemental financing. Freddie Mac allows borrowers to obtain supplemental loans after 12 months, enabling them to access equity created by property appreciation or debt paydown without refinancing the first mortgage.
  • Assumability. Freddie Mac loans are generally assumable, subject to lender and agency approval of the new borrower. This feature can be a significant selling point when marketing a property, as buyers can assume an existing below-market loan rather than originating new financing at higher rates.
  • Green incentives. The Green Up and Green Up Plus programs provide meaningful rate reductions for energy-efficient properties and improvements, creating a financial incentive to invest in sustainability.

Limitations

  • Multifamily only. Freddie Mac's multifamily programs are limited to residential rental properties with five or more units. Office, retail, industrial, and hospitality properties are not eligible. Mixed-use properties may qualify if the residential component represents the majority of the building.
  • Prepayment restrictions. Fixed-rate Freddie Mac loans typically require defeasance as the prepayment mechanism, which can be costly and complex. Borrowers who anticipate selling or refinancing before loan maturity should carefully evaluate the prepayment terms and consider floating-rate structures that offer more flexibility.
  • Stabilized properties preferred. Freddie Mac's conventional programs are designed for stabilized, well-occupied properties. Value-add transactions requiring significant renovation, properties with occupancy below 85%, and heavy construction projects are generally not eligible for conventional agency financing (though bridge-to-agency strategies can work).
  • Net worth and liquidity requirements. The borrower must demonstrate net worth equal to the loan amount and post-closing liquidity of at least 10% of the loan balance. These requirements can be challenging for newer investors or those with concentrated real estate holdings.
  • Limited lender network. With approximately 25 Optigo lenders, the network is smaller than the CMBS or bank lending universe. Borrowers in smaller markets may have fewer lender options, and competition for the best pricing may require outreach to multiple Optigo lenders.
  • Regulatory caps. FHFA sets annual volume caps for both Freddie Mac and Fannie Mae multifamily lending. While these caps have been increased over time and have generally not restricted loan availability, they can create uncertainty about agency lending capacity in periods of very high demand.

Frequently Asked Questions

Freddie Mac and Fannie Mae are both government-sponsored enterprises that provide liquidity for the multifamily housing market, but they operate through different lending networks and offer distinct program structures. Freddie Mac works through its Optigo lender network (formerly called Program Plus), which includes roughly 25 approved seller/servicers nationwide. Fannie Mae uses its Delegated Underwriting and Servicing (DUS) program, which includes a comparable number of approved lenders.

In terms of loan products, Freddie Mac tends to offer more flexibility on floating-rate structures and has a particularly strong Small Balance Loan program for deals between $750,000 and $7.5 million. Fannie Mae is often considered to have a slight edge on fixed-rate pricing for larger conventional deals and offers fixed-rate terms up to 30 years (compared to Freddie Mac's 20-year maximum). Both agencies offer competitive rates, non-recourse financing, and similar LTV and DSCR requirements.

The risk-sharing structures also differ. Freddie Mac's Optigo lenders share a portion of the credit risk on every loan they originate, while Fannie Mae's DUS lenders bear roughly one-third of any loss on the loans they underwrite. Both models are designed to incentivize strong underwriting discipline. In practice, the best choice depends on your specific property type, loan size, and the lender relationships available to you. Many borrowers request quotes from both agencies and compare the results.
