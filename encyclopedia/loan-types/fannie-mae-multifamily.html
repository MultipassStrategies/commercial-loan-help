Fannie Mae Multifamily Loans: DUS Program Rates, Terms, and Requirements

Commercial Loan Help Editorial Team March 17, 2026
Key Facts
  • Minimum loan: $1M+
  • Max LTV: 80%
  • Min DSCR: 1.25x
  • Term options: 5 to 30 years
  • Rate type: Fixed or floating
  • Amortization: 30 years

What Is the Fannie Mae DUS Program?

The Fannie Mae Delegated Underwriting and Servicing (DUS) program is the largest source of multifamily mortgage capital in the United States. Launched in 1988, it was designed to create a reliable, scalable channel for financing apartment properties by allowing approved lenders to underwrite, close, and service loans on Fannie Mae's behalf without requiring prior approval from Fannie Mae on each individual transaction. This delegated model is what gives the program its name, and it is the key reason DUS loans can close faster and more predictably than many competing agency products.

Fannie Mae does not lend directly to borrowers. Instead, it purchases and securitizes multifamily mortgages originated by its network of approved DUS lenders. By doing so, Fannie Mae provides liquidity to the multifamily lending market, enabling lenders to originate new loans continuously rather than holding every mortgage on their balance sheets. The resulting mortgage-backed securities are sold to institutional investors worldwide, and the steady demand for these securities keeps borrowing rates competitive.

The DUS program finances a wide range of multifamily property types, from conventional market-rate apartments to affordable housing, seniors housing, student housing, and manufactured housing communities. With loan amounts starting at $1 million and no hard upper limit (individual loans have exceeded $500 million), the program serves borrowers ranging from small local operators to large institutional real estate firms. Over its history, the DUS program has financed more than $1 trillion in multifamily mortgages, making it a cornerstone of the American rental housing market.

How DUS Lenders Work

The DUS model is built on a risk-sharing partnership between Fannie Mae and its approved lenders. Unlike many government-backed lending programs where the agency bears all the credit risk, DUS lenders retain a meaningful share of the loss exposure on every loan they originate. In the standard risk-sharing arrangement, the DUS lender absorbs the first 5% of any loss on a loan and shares in additional losses above that threshold, up to a maximum loss exposure of roughly one-third of the original loan amount. This structure gives lenders a powerful incentive to underwrite carefully, because poor loan performance directly affects their bottom line.

There are approximately 25 approved DUS lenders operating nationwide. These include major commercial banks, insurance company affiliates, and specialized mortgage banking firms. Some of the most active DUS lenders include Walker & Dunlop, Wells Fargo Multifamily Capital, Berkadia, CBRE Capital Markets, KeyBank Real Estate Capital, and Newmark. Each lender brings its own expertise, geographic focus, and relationship strengths, so selecting the right DUS lender for a given deal can have a material impact on execution, pricing, and the overall borrower experience.

Because DUS lenders have delegated authority, they can make credit decisions, set loan terms, and commit to rate locks without waiting for Fannie Mae to review and approve each loan. This streamlined process is a major advantage for borrowers. A DUS lender can typically issue a loan commitment within two to four weeks of receiving a complete application, and closings routinely occur within 45 to 60 days. For borrowers accustomed to the slower timelines of HUD or conduit (CMBS) lending, the DUS process can feel remarkably efficient.

Eligible Properties

The Fannie Mae DUS program is designed for stabilized multifamily properties, defined as residential rental properties with five or more units. The property must demonstrate a track record of stable occupancy (typically 90% or higher for at least 90 days prior to application) and generate sufficient net operating income to support the requested debt. Beyond conventional apartments, Fannie Mae finances several specialized property types within the multifamily category.

  • Market-rate apartments: Standard apartment communities of all sizes, from 5-unit buildings to large garden-style, mid-rise, and high-rise complexes. This is the most common property type in the DUS portfolio.
  • Affordable housing: Properties with rent restrictions, Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) allocations, Section 8 Housing Assistance Payment (HAP) contracts, or other regulatory agreements that limit rents to below-market levels. Fannie Mae actively supports affordable housing and offers pricing incentives for these properties.
  • Seniors housing: Independent living and assisted living communities that provide age-restricted rental housing. Memory care and skilled nursing facilities are generally not eligible under the standard DUS program.
  • Student housing: Purpose-built student housing located near college and university campuses. These properties must demonstrate strong demand and stable occupancy patterns tied to enrollment at accredited institutions.
  • Manufactured housing communities: Land-lease communities (also known as mobile home parks) where the borrower owns the land and infrastructure while residents own or rent the individual homes. These properties typically require a minimum of 50 pads.
  • Military housing: Privatized military housing communities operating under long-term ground leases with the U.S. Department of Defense.

Properties that are not eligible for DUS financing include hotels, motels, dormitories owned by educational institutions, nursing homes, hospitals, and single-family rental homes (unless part of a larger portfolio transaction). Mixed-use properties may qualify if the residential component constitutes at least 80% of the total gross income or square footage, but purely commercial buildings are excluded.

Fannie Mae Loan Programs

Fannie Mae offers several distinct loan products within the DUS platform, each designed to address different borrower needs, property profiles, and market conditions.

Standard Fixed-Rate Loans

The flagship product of the DUS program, standard fixed-rate loans provide long-term, non-recourse financing with fixed interest rates for terms ranging from 5 to 30 years. The most popular terms are 7, 10, and 12 years. These loans fully amortize over a 30-year schedule, and borrowers can request interest-only periods during the initial years of the term. Maximum leverage is 80% LTV with a minimum DSCR of 1.25x, though lower-leverage deals can receive more favorable pricing. Prepayment is governed by either a yield maintenance formula or a declining prepayment schedule, depending on borrower preference and the specific term selected.

Adjustable Rate Mortgages (ARM)

Fannie Mae's adjustable-rate products suit borrowers who anticipate holding a property for a shorter period or who want a lower initial rate. The Structured ARM (SARM) features a fixed-rate initial period (typically 5 to 10 years) followed by an adjustable period. The floating-rate option provides a variable rate tied to the Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) with monthly resets. ARM products typically offer greater prepayment flexibility than fixed-rate loans, often allowing payoff with minimal or no penalty after the initial fixed period.

Green Rewards

Available as an overlay on most DUS loan products, the Green Rewards program provides interest rate reductions and additional loan proceeds for borrowers who commit to energy and water efficiency improvements. This program is covered in detail in a later section of this guide.

Small Loans

The Fannie Mae Small Loan program targets properties with 5 to 50 units and loan amounts between $1 million and $6 million. It features streamlined underwriting, reduced documentation requirements, and faster processing compared to the standard DUS program. Small Loans offer fixed-rate terms from 5 to 30 years, with the same non-recourse structure as larger DUS loans. This program fills an important gap in the market, as many conventional lenders are reluctant to finance smaller apartment buildings due to the disproportionate cost of underwriting relative to loan size.

Structured Transactions

For larger or more complex deals, Fannie Mae offers structured transaction products including credit facilities (revolving lines secured by multiple properties), portfolio loans, and single-asset securitizations. These products are typically reserved for institutional borrowers with portfolios exceeding $50 million and require customized underwriting and documentation.

Rate Structure and Current Pricing

Fannie Mae DUS loan rates are expressed as a spread over the corresponding U.S. Treasury yield. For a 10-year fixed-rate loan, for example, the rate equals the 10-year Treasury yield plus a spread that typically ranges from 150 to 250 basis points, depending on leverage, property quality, market strength, and the borrower's overall profile. Lower-leverage deals and properties in strong markets generally receive tighter spreads, while higher-leverage transactions or properties with risk factors (such as secondary markets, smaller unit counts, or deferred maintenance) carry wider spreads.

The rate-setting process begins when the DUS lender quotes a spread to the borrower based on the underwritten deal. Once the borrower accepts the quote, the lender can lock the rate at any point during the process, typically at or near the time of application or commitment. The rate lock period is usually 30 to 60 days, though longer lock periods are available for an additional fee. Because the underlying Treasury yield moves daily, borrowers face interest rate risk between the time they receive a quote and the time they lock. Experienced borrowers and their advisors monitor Treasury movements closely and time their rate locks strategically.

For floating-rate products, pricing is expressed as a spread over SOFR. Typical all-in floating rates range from 200 to 350 basis points over SOFR, depending on leverage and deal characteristics. Floating-rate loans are popular among borrowers who plan a value-add strategy (renovating units and increasing rents) and expect to refinance into a fixed-rate product or sell the property within two to five years.

Qualification Requirements

Fannie Mae evaluates both the property and the borrower when underwriting a DUS loan. The standards are rigorous but straightforward, and borrowers who understand the requirements in advance can position their applications for the fastest possible execution.

Property Requirements

  • Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR): The property must generate a minimum DSCR of 1.25x, meaning net operating income must be at least 125% of the annual debt service. For affordable properties and certain other categories, slightly lower minimums may apply.
  • Loan-to-Value (LTV): Maximum LTV is 80% based on the lesser of the purchase price or appraised value. Lower LTV deals (65% to 75%) receive more favorable pricing and may qualify for extended interest-only periods.
  • Occupancy: The property must be at least 90% occupied for a minimum of 90 consecutive days prior to the loan application. Newly constructed properties may qualify under Fannie Mae's lease-up provisions if they meet specific criteria.
  • Condition: The property must be in good physical condition. Fannie Mae requires a property condition assessment (engineering report) to identify any deferred maintenance, structural issues, or capital expenditure needs. The lender will establish replacement reserve requirements based on the assessment findings.

Borrower Requirements

  • Net worth: The borrower (or the key principal) must have a net worth equal to or greater than the loan amount. Liquidity (cash and liquid assets) must generally equal at least 10% of the loan amount, though this threshold varies based on deal size and complexity.
  • Experience: Fannie Mae expects borrowers to have meaningful experience owning and managing multifamily properties. First-time multifamily borrowers may still qualify if they partner with an experienced operator or engage professional property management. The depth of experience required generally scales with the size and complexity of the deal.
  • Credit history: The key principal must have a satisfactory credit history with no recent bankruptcies, foreclosures, or deed-in-lieu events. While there is no published minimum credit score, DUS lenders typically look for scores above 680 and review the borrower's full credit profile for patterns of responsible financial management.
  • Entity structure: The borrowing entity is typically a single-purpose entity (SPE), usually structured as a limited liability company or limited partnership. This structure isolates the property from the borrower's other assets and liabilities, which is a standard requirement for non-recourse commercial real estate loans.

Key Benefits

Fannie Mae DUS loans offer a combination of features that make them among the most attractive financing options available for multifamily property owners.

  • Non-recourse financing: DUS loans are non-recourse to the borrower, meaning the lender's remedy in the event of default is limited to the property itself. The borrower's personal assets are not at risk, with the exception of standard "bad boy" carve-outs for fraud, misrepresentation, environmental liability, and other specified acts. This structure is a significant advantage over small-balance bank loans, which almost always require a full personal guarantee.
  • Interest-only options: Borrowers can request interest-only periods during the initial years of the loan term. For lower-leverage deals, full-term interest-only is available, meaning the borrower pays only interest for the entire loan term and repays the full principal at maturity. Interest-only periods improve cash-on-cash returns during the hold period and are especially popular among value-add investors.
  • Supplemental loans: After 12 months, borrowers can take out a supplemental (second lien) loan on a property with an existing Fannie Mae first mortgage, allowing them to access appreciated equity without refinancing the original loan. This is particularly valuable when the original loan carries a below-market rate that the borrower wants to preserve.
  • Assumability: DUS loans are assumable, meaning a qualified buyer can take over the existing loan when the property is sold. Assumable loans can be a major selling point in a rising rate environment, as the buyer inherits the original loan's rate and terms. The assuming borrower must meet Fannie Mae's standard underwriting requirements and pay an assumption fee, typically 1% of the outstanding loan balance.
  • Long terms with 30-year amortization: Fixed-rate terms up to 30 years combined with 30-year amortization schedules produce low, predictable monthly payments. Longer terms reduce refinancing risk and give borrowers stability over the full hold period.
  • Rate certainty: Early rate locks allow borrowers to eliminate interest rate risk well before closing. This is especially important during periods of Treasury volatility, when rates can move significantly in a short time.
  • No ongoing financial reporting for Small Loans: Borrowers with loans under the Small Loan program benefit from reduced ongoing reporting requirements compared to larger DUS loans, which simplifies administration for smaller operators.

Limitations and Considerations

Despite its many advantages, the Fannie Mae DUS program has limitations that borrowers should evaluate carefully before committing to this financing path.

  • No construction or bridge financing: The DUS program finances only stabilized properties. It does not provide construction loans, bridge loans for properties in lease-up, or financing for gut renovations that take a property offline. Borrowers pursuing ground-up development or heavy value-add projects must obtain separate construction or bridge financing and then refinance into a DUS loan once the property is stabilized.
  • Minimum property size: While the Small Loan program accommodates properties as small as 5 units, some DUS lenders set internal minimums of $1 million or $3 million, making it difficult to finance the smallest apartment buildings. Borrowers with properties below these thresholds may need to explore alternative programs or lenders with lower minimums.
  • Prepayment restrictions: Fixed-rate DUS loans carry prepayment provisions that can be costly if the borrower needs to pay off the loan early. Yield maintenance (the most common prepayment structure) requires the borrower to compensate the lender for the lost interest income, which can be substantial if rates have fallen since origination. Declining prepayment schedules (such as a step-down from 5% to 1% over the final five years of the term) offer more predictability but may still be significant. Borrowers who anticipate a sale or refinance before maturity should negotiate prepayment terms carefully at origination.
  • Replacement reserves: Fannie Mae requires borrowers to establish replacement reserve accounts, funded through monthly deposits, to cover future capital expenditures such as roof replacement, HVAC systems, and common area upgrades. The reserve amount is determined by the property condition assessment and typically ranges from $250 to $350 per unit per year for standard properties, with higher amounts for older buildings or those with identified deferred maintenance.
  • Recourse carve-outs: While DUS loans are non-recourse, the key principal signs a guaranty covering specific "bad boy" acts such as fraud, intentional misrepresentation, misapplication of rents, environmental violations, and voluntary bankruptcy filing. These carve-outs are standard in agency lending, but borrowers should understand exactly what triggers recourse liability.
  • Geographic and market sensitivity: Fannie Mae adjusts its underwriting standards based on market conditions. Properties in secondary or tertiary markets may face lower maximum LTV ratios, wider pricing spreads, or additional reserve requirements compared to properties in primary markets with strong fundamentals.

The Application Process

Securing a Fannie Mae DUS loan follows a structured process that, while thorough, is generally faster and more predictable than HUD or CMBS financing. Here is what borrowers can expect at each stage.

  1. Lender selection. The first step is identifying and engaging a DUS lender. Because each lender brings different strengths (geographic expertise, property type specialization, pricing competitiveness, and service quality), borrowers benefit from comparing multiple lender quotes. A commercial loan marketplace like Commercial Loan Help can facilitate this process by matching borrowers with DUS lenders who are actively lending on similar properties in the same market.
  2. Preliminary quote and term sheet. The DUS lender reviews basic property and borrower information (rent roll, operating statements, property photos, borrower financial summary) and issues a preliminary quote or term sheet outlining the proposed loan amount, rate, term, and key conditions. This step typically takes one to two weeks.
  3. Application and due diligence. Once the borrower accepts the term sheet, the formal application process begins. The borrower submits a complete package including three years of property operating statements, a current rent roll, borrower and guarantor financial statements, organizational documents, and information about property management. The lender simultaneously orders third-party reports: an appraisal, a property condition assessment, a Phase I environmental site assessment, and a seismic report if the property is in a seismic zone.
  4. Underwriting. The DUS lender's underwriting team analyzes the property's income and expenses, market position, physical condition, and environmental risk. They also evaluate the borrower's financial strength, experience, and credit history. Because DUS lenders have delegated authority, underwriting decisions are made internally without submitting the file to Fannie Mae for review (except for very large or unusual transactions that exceed the lender's delegated limits).
  5. Rate lock. The borrower can lock the interest rate at any point after the lender is comfortable with the underwriting. Many borrowers choose to lock at or around the time of commitment, when the lender issues a formal loan commitment letter. Rate lock periods are typically 30 to 60 days, with extensions available if needed.
  6. Closing. After underwriting is complete and all conditions are satisfied, the loan closes. The DUS lender funds the loan and simultaneously sells it to Fannie Mae, which pools it into a mortgage-backed security. The DUS lender retains servicing responsibilities and remains the borrower's primary point of contact for the life of the loan.

From application to closing, the standard DUS process takes approximately 45 to 60 days. The Small Loan program can close in 30 to 45 days thanks to its streamlined underwriting. Borrowers can accelerate timelines by preparing complete documentation packages in advance and responding promptly to lender information requests.

Fannie Mae vs. Freddie Mac Comparison

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are both government-sponsored enterprises (GSEs) that provide liquidity to the multifamily lending market, but they operate through different lender networks and offer somewhat different program features. The table below highlights the key differences borrowers should consider when choosing between the two agencies.

Lender network ~25 approved DUS lenders ~25 approved Optigo lenders

Green/sustainability program Green Rewards Green Advantage

In practice, the two agencies compete directly for the same borrowers and properties, and their pricing is often very close. The most significant differences tend to be at the lender level rather than the agency level: a particular DUS lender may offer better execution on a given deal than an Optigo lender (or vice versa) based on their appetite for the property type, market, or deal size. Borrowers who solicit quotes from multiple lenders across both platforms are well positioned to secure the best available terms.

Green Rewards Program

Fannie Mae's Green Rewards program is one of the most successful sustainability-linked lending initiatives in commercial real estate. It incentivizes multifamily property owners to reduce energy and water consumption by offering lower interest rates and additional loan proceeds for properties that meet specific efficiency improvement targets.

How It Works

To participate in Green Rewards, the borrower must commit to reducing the property's energy or water consumption by at least 30% (measured against a baseline established through utility benchmarking or an energy audit). The borrower receives two primary benefits: a reduction in the interest rate spread, typically 10 to 15 basis points below the standard DUS rate, and the ability to finance the cost of green improvements with additional loan proceeds of up to 5% of the total loan amount.

Eligible Improvements

Green Rewards financing can cover a broad range of energy and water efficiency upgrades, including LED lighting conversions, high-efficiency HVAC systems, low-flow plumbing fixtures, smart thermostats and building automation systems, insulation and air sealing, Energy Star appliances, solar panel installations, and water-efficient landscaping (xeriscaping). The specific improvements are identified through a property assessment conducted by a qualified energy auditor or through a recognized green building certification process.

Verification and Compliance

Borrowers must complete all committed improvements within 12 months of loan closing. Fannie Mae verifies results through post-renovation utility benchmarking, comparing actual consumption data against the pre-renovation baseline. If the property fails to achieve the committed reduction, the borrower may be required to fund additional improvements or face adjustments to the loan terms. In practice, most Green Rewards borrowers meet or exceed their targets, because the required improvements are identified by professional auditors who base their projections on well-established engineering calculations.

The Green Rewards program is available as an overlay on most DUS loan products, including standard fixed-rate, adjustable-rate, and Small Loan transactions. Since its launch, the program has financed billions of dollars in green improvements across thousands of multifamily properties, making it one of the largest sources of private capital for residential energy efficiency in the country.

The supplemental loan is a separate, subordinate lien that sits behind the first mortgage, and it does not require the borrower to refinance or modify the original loan. The underwriting and closing process for supplemental loans is generally faster than for new originations, since the lender already has a relationship with the borrower and familiarity with the property.
