What Is DSCR?

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) is the single most important metric in commercial real estate underwriting. It measures whether a property generates enough income to cover its mortgage payments. Lenders use this ratio to determine how much risk they are taking when making a loan.

In simple terms, DSCR answers one question: can this property pay for itself? If the answer is yes (and by a comfortable margin), lenders are more likely to approve the loan and offer favorable terms.

How the Calculation Works

The DSCR formula has two components:

DSCR = Net Operating Income (NOI) / Annual Debt Service

Net Operating Income (NOI) is calculated by taking your gross rental income, subtracting vacancy losses, and then subtracting all operating expenses. Operating expenses include property management fees, insurance, property taxes, maintenance, utilities, and repairs. NOI does not include debt service (mortgage payments), depreciation, or capital expenditures.

Annual Debt Service is the total amount you pay toward the mortgage each year, including both principal and interest. If you know your monthly payment, simply multiply by 12.

For example, if a property has $120,000 in gross rental income, 5% vacancy ($6,000 loss), and $40,000 in operating expenses:

  • Effective Gross Income: $120,000 minus $6,000 = $114,000
  • NOI: $114,000 minus $40,000 = $74,000
  • If annual debt service is $56,000, then DSCR = $74,000 / $56,000 = 1.32x

What Lenders Look For

Different lenders and loan programs have different DSCR thresholds, but here is a general guide:

DSCR Range Rating Lender Perspective
1.40x and above Excellent Strongest terms, lowest rates, widest lender pool
1.25x to 1.39x Strong Meets most lender requirements comfortably
1.15x to 1.24x Adequate Some lenders will proceed; may require additional reserves or lower LTV
1.00x to 1.14x Marginal Limited conventional options; may need bridge or private credit
Below 1.00x Insufficient Property does not cover debt service; restructuring needed

In 2026, DSCR has become even more critical. Many properties that were originated with comfortable DSCRs at low rates now face tighter ratios when refinancing at higher rates. This is one of the core challenges driving the $875 billion maturity wall.

DSCR Requirements by Loan Type

  • Conventional Bank Loans: Typically require 1.20x to 1.25x minimum
  • CMBS Loans: Generally 1.25x or higher
  • Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac (Multifamily): 1.20x to 1.25x depending on the program
  • HUD/FHA Loans: 1.18x to 1.20x (lower threshold due to government insurance)
  • SBA Loans: 1.25x global DSCR (includes all income and debt, not just the property)
  • Bridge Loans: Often 1.0x to 1.10x (more flexible due to short-term nature)
  • Private Credit: Varies widely; some accept below 1.0x with strong sponsor guarantees

Tips to Improve Your DSCR

If your DSCR is lower than you need, consider these strategies:

  • Increase income: Raise rents to market level, add revenue sources (parking, laundry, storage), or reduce vacancy through better marketing and tenant retention.
  • Reduce operating expenses: Renegotiate service contracts, improve energy efficiency, or audit your insurance and property tax assessments.
  • Put more equity down: A smaller loan means lower debt service, which directly improves DSCR.
  • Extend the amortization period: A 30-year amortization produces lower annual payments than a 20-year schedule on the same loan amount.
  • Negotiate a lower interest rate: Even small rate improvements can meaningfully change your DSCR. Shopping multiple lenders is essential.
  • Use an interest-only period: Some lenders offer 1 to 3 years of interest-only payments, which temporarily boosts DSCR while you stabilize the property.
  • Consider mezzanine or preferred equity: If senior debt DSCR is tight, additional capital from the mezzanine or preferred equity layer can reduce the senior loan amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most conventional lenders require a minimum DSCR of 1.20x to 1.25x. This means the property needs to generate 20 to 25% more income than the annual mortgage payment. A DSCR of 1.40x or higher is considered excellent and will unlock the widest range of lender options and the best terms. For bridge loans and private credit, some lenders will accept lower DSCRs (even below 1.0x in certain cases) if the borrower has a strong plan for stabilization.

DSCR and LTV measure different things. DSCR measures a property's ability to generate enough income to cover debt payments (income divided by debt service). LTV measures the loan amount relative to the property's value (loan amount divided by property value). Both metrics are used by lenders to size and approve loans. A deal can have strong LTV but weak DSCR (or vice versa), and lenders typically require both metrics to meet their thresholds before approving a loan.

A DSCR below 1.0x means the property cannot cover its debt payments from operating income alone. Most conventional lenders will not approve a loan in this situation. However, some options may be available: bridge loans for value-add properties where income will increase after renovation, hard money loans that focus on asset value rather than income, and private credit lenders who consider the borrower's overall financial strength. You may also consider putting more equity into the deal to bring the loan amount (and therefore debt service) down to a level where DSCR exceeds 1.0x.

Property taxes and insurance are included in the operating expenses you subtract from income to calculate NOI (Net Operating Income). They are not part of debt service. Debt service refers only to the mortgage payment (principal and interest). Some lenders also calculate an "all-in" DSCR that includes taxes and insurance in the debt service figure, but the standard industry calculation uses NOI (after taxes and insurance are deducted from income) divided by mortgage payments only. This calculator uses the standard method.

Lenders may calculate NOI differently than you do. Common reasons for discrepancies include: the lender applying a management fee even if you self-manage, the lender using their own vacancy and credit loss assumptions, adjustments for below-market rents or above-market expenses, and the inclusion of replacement reserves. Some lenders also "stress test" the DSCR at a higher hypothetical interest rate to ensure the property can handle rate increases. This calculator uses the inputs you provide, so your results will vary depending on the assumptions you use.
