Understanding Commercial Mortgage Payments

Commercial mortgage payments are calculated using the same core formula as residential mortgages, but with a critical distinction: commercial loans almost always have a shorter term than the amortization period. This means the loan is paid down over 25 or 30 years on paper, but the full remaining balance comes due as a "balloon payment" at the end of the loan term (typically 5, 7, or 10 years).

The standard formula for a fixed-rate amortizing loan payment is: M = P[r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1], where P is the principal loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12), and n is the total number of monthly payments over the amortization period.

Amortization vs. Loan Term: The Balloon Payment

One of the most important concepts in commercial lending is the difference between amortization and loan term. The amortization period (typically 25 to 30 years) determines your monthly payment amount by spreading principal repayment over a long horizon. The loan term (typically 5 to 10 years) is the actual duration of the loan before the remaining balance must be repaid or refinanced.

For example, a $1,500,000 loan with a 25-year amortization but a 10-year term will have low monthly payments based on the 25-year schedule. However, at the end of year 10, the borrower must pay off the remaining balance in full. This remaining balance is called the balloon payment. In this example, the balloon payment would still be well over $1 million because only 10 years of the 25-year amortization schedule have been completed.

The 2026 maturity wall exists precisely because of this structure. Borrowers who took out 5-, 7-, or 10-year term loans between 2016 and 2021 now face balloon payments at a time when refinancing conditions are less favorable.

Fully amortizing loans (where the term equals the amortization period) do exist. HUD/FHA multifamily loans, for instance, offer 35-year fully amortizing terms with no balloon payment. However, these are the exception in commercial lending.

Factors That Affect Your Commercial Mortgage Rate

Several key variables influence the interest rate a lender will offer:

  • Loan-to-Value (LTV): Lower LTV means less risk for the lender and typically better rates. Most conventional commercial loans cap at 65 to 75% LTV.
  • Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR): Lenders want to see at least 1.20 to 1.25x DSCR. Stronger DSCR often leads to better pricing.
  • Property type: Multifamily and industrial properties generally receive the most competitive rates. Hospitality and office carry more risk and typically higher pricing.
  • Borrower experience and credit: Experienced sponsors with strong credit and net worth get better terms.
  • Loan type: SBA, agency (Fannie/Freddie), and HUD loans often have the most competitive rates. Bridge, hard money, and private credit loans carry higher rates in exchange for speed and flexibility.
  • Market conditions: Federal Reserve policy, Treasury yields, SOFR rates, and the overall economic environment all influence commercial lending rates.
  • Fixed vs. floating: Fixed rates provide certainty but may be higher initially. Floating rates (SOFR plus a spread) may start lower but carry the risk of rate increases.

How to Reduce Your Monthly Payment

If your estimated payment is higher than you expected, consider these strategies:

  • Increase the down payment: A larger down payment reduces the principal, which directly reduces monthly payments and may also qualify you for a lower rate.
  • Extend the amortization period: Moving from 20-year to 25-year or 30-year amortization reduces monthly payments (though you pay more total interest over time).
  • Choose an interest-only period: Some commercial loans offer 1 to 3 years of interest-only payments at the beginning of the loan, significantly reducing initial payment obligations.
  • Shop multiple lenders: Rates and terms vary significantly across lender types. Our free ClearPath matching system can connect you with multiple lenders in 60 seconds to compare options.
  • Consider loan type: SBA 504 loans offer below-market fixed rates on the CDC portion. Agency loans (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) offer competitive rates for multifamily. HUD loans offer the lowest rates and longest terms for qualifying properties.

Using This Calculator Effectively

This calculator is designed to give you a quick estimate of your commercial mortgage payment. For the most accurate results:

  • Enter the actual property price and your expected down payment percentage to auto-calculate the loan amount and see your LTV.
  • Use the amortization period your lender has quoted (25 years is the most common for commercial real estate).
  • Set the loan term to match the actual term being offered (not the amortization). This is critical for seeing your balloon payment amount.
  • The Annual Debt Service figure is useful for calculating DSCR: divide your property's Net Operating Income (NOI) by the Annual Debt Service to get your DSCR.

Keep in mind that this calculator assumes a fixed interest rate and standard amortization. Actual payments may include escrows for taxes, insurance, and reserves, which add to your total monthly obligation.
