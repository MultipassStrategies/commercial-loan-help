How to Compare Commercial Loan Offers

When you receive multiple loan offers for a commercial property, the interest rate is the first number most borrowers look at. While rate is important, it is only one piece of the picture. The true cost of a loan includes origination fees, closing costs, annual servicing fees, and the structure of the loan itself. Two loans at the same rate can have very different total costs depending on these additional factors.

This comparison tool is designed to help you evaluate up to three loan offers by calculating the metrics that matter most. By entering the details of each offer, you can see a clear side-by-side breakdown that highlights which loan is the best fit for your situation.

Key Metrics to Compare

When evaluating commercial loan offers, focus on these five metrics:

  • Monthly Payment: The amount you will pay each month for principal and interest. A lower monthly payment improves cash flow but may result from a longer amortization (which means more total interest and a larger balloon payment).
  • Total Interest Over the Term: The cumulative interest you will pay from origination through the end of the loan term. This does not include fees or closing costs.
  • Total Cost of Capital: The single most telling number. This adds total interest, origination fees, annual fees over the full term, and closing costs into one figure. It represents the all-in cost of the loan.
  • Effective Annual Rate: The true annualized cost of borrowing when all fees and costs are factored in. This allows you to compare loans with different fee structures on an apples-to-apples basis.
  • Balloon Payment at Maturity: The remaining balance when the loan term expires. If the term is shorter than the amortization period, you will need to refinance or sell to cover this amount.
Total Cost of Capital = Total Interest + Origination Fee + (Annual Fees x Term) + Closing Costs

What to Look for Beyond the Interest Rate

Experienced borrowers know that rate is important but not everything. Here are additional factors to weigh when comparing offers:

  • Prepayment penalties: Some loans (especially CMBS) carry steep prepayment costs through yield maintenance or defeasance. If you plan to sell or refinance before maturity, these penalties can add significant expense.
  • Recourse vs. non-recourse: A non-recourse loan limits your personal liability, which has value even if the rate is slightly higher. Weigh the risk protection against the cost difference.
  • Rate lock period: How long is the rate guaranteed before closing? A longer lock protects you against rate increases during the closing process.
  • Flexibility: Can you make additional principal payments? Is there an interest-only option? Can the loan be assumed by a buyer? These features can affect your total cost and exit strategy.
  • Lender reputation and speed: A slightly more expensive loan from a reliable lender who closes on time can be worth more than a cheaper offer from a lender who may not perform.

Putting It All Together

The best loan for your deal is not always the one with the lowest rate. It is the one with the lowest total cost of capital, the most favorable terms, and the structure that matches your business plan. If you are holding the property long-term, total interest and annual fees matter most. If you plan to sell or refinance within a few years, focus on upfront costs, prepayment flexibility, and the balloon payment amount.

Use this tool to run the numbers, then consider the qualitative factors listed above. The combination of quantitative analysis and practical judgment will lead you to the right decision. Total cost of capital is the sum of all expenses associated with borrowing, including total interest paid over the loan term, origination fees, annual fees for the life of the term, and estimated closing costs. This metric gives you a true picture of what a loan will cost, because two loans with the same interest rate can have very different total costs depending on their fee structures.

The effective annual rate accounts for not just the stated interest rate but also the impact of fees and closing costs spread over the loan term. It represents the true annual cost of borrowing when all costs are included. A loan with a lower stated rate but high fees may actually have a higher effective rate than a loan with a slightly higher stated rate and lower fees.

Not necessarily. The lowest interest rate does not always mean the lowest total cost. Origination fees, closing costs, annual servicing fees, and the loan term all affect your total expense. A loan at 7.0% with 2 points in origination and $15,000 in closing costs may cost more over 10 years than a loan at 7.25% with no origination fee and $5,000 in closing costs. Always compare total cost of capital and effective annual rate.

The amortization period determines your monthly payment amount. A longer amortization (such as 30 years versus 20 years) results in lower monthly payments but more total interest over the loan term. It also means a larger balloon payment at maturity because less principal has been paid down. When comparing loans, make sure you understand how different amortization periods affect both your monthly cash flow and your total cost.

A balloon payment is the remaining loan balance due at the end of the loan term when the term is shorter than the amortization period. This is standard in commercial lending. For example, a loan with a 25-year amortization and 10-year term will have a large balance due after 10 years. When comparing loans, consider the balloon payment size because it represents the amount you will need to refinance or pay at maturity. A smaller balloon payment reduces your refinancing risk.
