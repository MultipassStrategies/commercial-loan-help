Why This Problem Is Bigger Than You Think

Small and medium-sized businesses are the engine of the American economy — responsible for nearly two-thirds of net new jobs created over the past two decades. Yet there is a $130 billion lending gap between what small businesses need and what traditional banks are willing to provide. Traditional underwriting models penalize borrowers who lack collateral, long credit histories, or the kind of tidy financials that fit neatly into an automated approval system. The result is that viable businesses with real revenue and real customers are told "no" by the very institutions that should be funding them.

SBA Loans Explained: Which Program Is Right for You?

SBA 7(a) Loan

Up to $5 Million | Flexible Use

The SBA 7(a) loan is the flagship loan program of the U.S. Small Business Administration. It provides up to $5 million in financing for a wide range of business purposes — including working capital, equipment purchases, real estate acquisition, business acquisition, and debt refinancing. The SBA does not lend directly; instead, it guarantees up to 85% of the loan amount (for loans up to $150,000) or 75% (for loans above $150,000), reducing the lender's risk and making it possible for borrowers who might not otherwise qualify for conventional financing to access capital. SBA 7(a) loans are available through SBA-approved lenders including banks, credit unions, and non-bank lending institutions. Terms can extend up to 25 years for real estate and up to 10 years for working capital.

SBA 504 Loan

Fixed Rate | Real Estate & Equipment

The SBA 504 loan program is designed specifically for major fixed asset purchases — primarily commercial real estate and heavy equipment. What makes 504 loans unique is their tripartite structure: a private-sector lender provides 50% of the financing, a Certified Development Company (CDC) provides up to 40% (backed by an SBA-guaranteed debenture), and the borrower contributes as little as 10% as a down payment. This structure results in below-market, fixed interest rates on the CDC portion of the loan, making it one of the most affordable long-term financing options available to small businesses. 504 loans can be used for purchasing land and buildings, constructing new facilities, renovating existing properties, or purchasing long-term machinery and equipment.

Can't Qualify for SBA? There's Still a Path.

Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs) are federally certified, mission-driven lenders that exist to serve communities and borrowers that traditional banks underserve. CDFIs include community banks, credit unions, loan funds, and venture capital funds that have been certified by the U.S. Department of the Treasury's CDFI Fund. Unlike conventional lenders, CDFIs evaluate borrowers holistically — weighing factors like community impact, business potential, and owner character alongside traditional financial metrics. Because they receive federal funding and tax credit allocations, CDFIs can offer lower interest rates (averaging around 7% compared to 11%+ from online alternative lenders) and higher approval rates than both banks and non-bank online lenders. For small business owners who have been turned down by banks and do not qualify for SBA programs, CDFIs represent a legitimate, affordable, and often overlooked path to capital.

SBA lenders look at your overall financial picture — including revenue trends, owner experience, collateral, and a reasonable projection of future cash flow. Startups and pre-revenue businesses can qualify in some cases, especially with strong owner resumes, adequate collateral, or participation in SBA microloans and Community Advantage programs. If you've been turned down for profitability reasons, there are still paths forward.

Most SBA lenders look for a minimum personal credit score between 620 and 640. However, the score alone does not determine approval — lenders also evaluate business financials, cash flow, industry experience, and collateral. A score of 680 or above typically qualifies you for the best rates and terms. If your score is below 620, CDFI lenders and microloan programs may still be available. The State Small Business Credit Initiative (SSBCI) 2.0 is a $10 billion federal program created under the American Rescue Plan Act that provides funding to states, territories, and tribal governments to support small business lending and investment programs. Each state administers its own SSBCI program differently — some offer loan participation programs, others provide collateral support or venture capital funding. SSBCI funds are specifically targeted to underserved communities and businesses that have difficulty accessing traditional capital.
